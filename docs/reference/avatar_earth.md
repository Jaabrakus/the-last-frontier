# Earth: how it is gathered and shaped (reference)

**Sources and honesty.** Technique names, users and episodes come from the Avatar Wiki's Earthbending page and its

## 1. The art behind it

- Earth is Hung Gar: "heavily rooted stances and strong kicks and punches that evoke the mass and power of earth",
  with tiger-hard blows and crane-soft landings. Toph is the exception: Southern Praying Mantis, narrow high stances,
  "touch and hit", precise stepping that keeps her in constant contact with the ground so she can "see" through it.
  (Wiki: Connection; Sifu Kisu: Toph's style used finger movements for her rock spikes and "a lot of Toph's stuff
  was really classical".)
- Principle: neutral jing, "waiting and listening for the right moment to strike and, when that moment comes, acting
  decisively"; earthbenders take the hit and counter. Earth balances offence and defence more evenly than the others.
  (Wiki: Fighting style, Spirituality; "Return to Omashu", "Bitter Work".)
- Strongest with the feet on the ground ("reliance on the lower body"), often barefoot; weak when cut off from earth.
  The opposite of air: root versus circle.
- Pro-bending earthbenders root only long enough to launch a disc, then move (wiki: Fighting style).

## 2. How earth is gathered

The earth is almost always **already there**: the bender never conjures it, they pull, lift, tear or step it into
place. From the wiki and memory:

- **Plant the stance first.** Feet wide, knees bent, weight low (horse stance), hands at the hips or on guard. The
  stance is the gather: it is the anticipation, 6-10 frames (0.25-0.4 s) of dropping and settling. Toph's is
  narrower and higher, hands feeling forwards.
- **Stomp or stamp.** A foot driven down sends a shock into the ground: the ground cracks in a line outwards, dust
  jumps, and the cracked edge rises as a slab. The wiki: "Striking the ground with feet, fists ... creates localized
  earthquakes or fissures"; "Earth launch": quickly moving rock beneath the feet to be thrown tens of feet up
  (Toph and Bumi). Typical: stomp snap 2-3 frames.
- **Pull a rock out of the ground.** A hand drawn up in a fist or open palm, rising from the hip, and the rock peels
  up with soil falling away from its underside, then hangs a beat. The wiki's "earth and stone levitation": "levitating
  nearby pieces of earth and stone ... propelling them at foes with punching or kicking motions" (Haru in "Imprisoned").
  Rock rises 8-12 frames; the punch or kick that throws it is 2 frames.
- **Cut a slab from a wall or cliff.** A slicing palm edge, a kick or a twist, and a block shears off, then slides or
  tips free (rock slides, stair folding in "The Earth King", slab shackles).
- **Compress or break.** Squeeze several stones into one (the wiki: Haru and Tyro with coal) or smash a rock with a
  punch or kick into a spray of fragments that are then flung (earth smash).
- **Raise a whole feature.** Both arms rising together brings up a wall or pillar (earth wall, rock column, earth
  elevator, earth tent); a palm slap brings a ripple through the ground (earth preservation, Jianzhu).
- **Sand, mud, metal.** Sand is gathered by flowing arm sweeps (sandbenders: a sand spout behind their sailers); mud
  is thick water with earth in it; metal is "bent" by finding the earth inside it (Toph, "The Guru").
- **Seismic sense** (the other half of gathering): the bender reads the ground first; in the show this is a still
  moment with feet bare and head tilted, often a cut to a ripple outline on the ground (memory; "The Blind Bandit",
  "The Chase").
- **Armour** is gathered inward: rocks, dust, pebbles or crystals pulled round the body and moulded to fit (earth
  armour); Aang's crystal armour grows from the cells outward (memory; "The Crossroads of Destiny").

## 3. The shapes of the classic moves

"Ours" is the game's move the shape maps to (section 7).

- **Boulder throw / rock projectile** (ours: Pebbles for the small version). A rock pulled up, held a beat at hip or
  shoulder height with dust falling off it, then driven out by a punch or kick; it travels straight, spinning a
  little, trailing a wedge of dust and chips (memory). On a hit it bursts into fragments and a dust ring. Rock
  rises 8-12 frames, hold 4-6, throw 2, flight 12-20.
- **Rock column / pillar** (ours: Pillar). Pulled up by a rising fist or an upward kick; the pillar shoots up in 4-6
  frames with a dust burst and soil at its base; used to launch the bender (earth launch), block, knock an enemy up.
  The wiki: "limited to the ground and does not have the same range as a rock projectile". Ends by sinking back or
  being left standing; a broken pillar drops as blocks.
- **Earth wall** (ours: Earth Wall). Both arms rise (or one hand sweeps across) and a slab lifts in front, edges
  ragged on top; reads as a rectangle with strata lines; dust on the rise. Used offence and defence; Aang's wall vs
  Azula; Toph's shelter. It ends cracked and crumbling after a hit, or the bender lowers it.
- **Rock shield** (ours: shield over a carry). A levitated slab held in front of the bender, "can be hurled at the
  opponent for quick retaliation" (wiki).
- **Earth gauntlet / rock gloves / rock armour** (ours: Stone Form). Rocks gathered on the fists, the whole body or
  the shoulders: the gauntlet "grants some level of the protection of earth armor but allows the rest of the body to
  remain flexible" (wiki; Aang vs Azula on the drill). The Dai Li's rock gloves are fired or kept as detachable
  hands that grab (wiki; "Lake Laogai").
- **Fissure / earthquake** (ours: Fissure). A stomp or ground-punch; a crack races along the ground from the bender
  to the target, edges lifting, dust spurting along the line; the target drops or is thrown off balance. "More
  advanced earthbenders can make narrow fissures for precise attacks." The crack stays as a scar (memory).
- **Earth wave / earth skating** (ours: Burrow, the nearest). A wave of earth that the bender rides or sends out as an
  attack (Aang and Toph in "The Crossroads of Destiny"; General Fong and Aang in "The Avatar State"). Reads as a
  rolling ridge of ground with dust behind it (memory).
- **Earth tunneling** (ours: Burrow). Pulling the earth past the body to swim through it (wiki; Bumi, Toph).
- **Earth pull / grab** (ours: Earth Grip). Slab shackles bind wrists or ankles with triangular slabs (Aang vs Ozai,
  Lin and Su vs Zaheer); rock cuffs; sinking an opponent to the knees (Master Yu, General Fong).
- **Catch** (ours: Catch). Stopping a thrown rock with a palm, or pulling it out of the air and into a block
  (the wiki: levitation and shields; memory for how the show draws it: the rock slows into the palm, dust falls off).
- **Dust cloud, summon the storm.** A shake of the ground raises cover; small gravel, dust and sand swirl round the
  bender (wiki, master level).
- **Mend / Patch** (ours: Patch, Mend). The show has no earth healing; use earth re-forming: cracks closing,
  rubble flowing back into a wall (the wiki's earth wall and shelter show the material re-set at will).

## 4. Timing at a glance (typical 2D figures)

| Move | Gather | Release | Travel | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boulder | stance drop 6-10 frames, rock rises 8-12, hold 4-6 | punch or kick 2 frames | 12-20 frames, dust trail | bursts into chips, dust ring |
| Pillar | stance drop 4-6 | rise 4-6 frames | stationary | sinks back 12-20 or left; blocks drop if broken |
| Wall | both arms rise 8-12 | settles 2-3 frames | stationary | cracks, crumbles, or lowered |
| Fissure | stomp wind-up 6-8 | stomp 2-3 frames | crack runs 10-16 frames | scar stays, dust drifts |
| Slab throw | cut and lift 10-14 | 2 frames | 10-16 frames | shatters |
| Gauntlet | rocks fly to the fists 6-10 | instant | n/a | crumbles away when dropped |

## 5. Reference moments (episode pages are `https://avatar.fandom.com/wiki/<Title_With_Underscores>`)

1. **"The Blind Bandit"** (2.6). Toph's tournament fights: rock column, sinking an opponent, spikes from finger
   movements, seismic sense. Look at: her low open stance, small hand motions, the "touch and hit" economy.
2. **"Imprisoned"** (1.6). Haru and his father compress small pieces of coal into one boulder (wiki: needs two
   benders, so a hard move); Haru's rock work (memory). Look at: several small pieces drawn into one mass.
3. **"The King of Omashu"** (1.5). Bumi's duel with Aang, where (wiki) Aang's air column throws aside a large boulder.
   Look at: a big rock deflected by a spinning column, and how the rock's weight is drawn.
4. **"Bitter Work"** (2.9). Toph teaches Aang; the earth armour training exercise; earth etching with two fingers.
   Look at: patient stance work, armour gathered rock by rock, a line drawn in stone.
5. **"The Drill"** (2.13). Toph and Aang's earth elevator carrying a family and Appa; Aang's earth gauntlet vs Azula;
   Toph pushes the slurry back. Look at: a slab rising smooth and level, and the contrast of gauntlet and fire.
6. **"The Desert"** (2.11) and **"The Library"** (2.10). The sandbenders' sand spout behind their sailers; Toph's
   earth impression ("snow angel") on the Si Wong Rock. Look at: sand drawn as a flowing ribbon, the body pressed
   into stone.
7. **"Lake Laogai"** (2.17). The Dai Li's rock gloves and shoes, wall-clinging. Look at: rock shaped like a hand,
   thrown and retracted.
8. **"The Avatar State"** (2.1). General Fong's earth wave and sinking; Aang's wave. Look at: a rolling ridge of
   ground at army scale.
9. **"The Crossroads of Destiny"** (2.20). Aang's crystal armour vs Azula; Aang's earth bomb on Zuko; earth skating.
   Look at: faceted crystal growing outward in steps; rock thrown at the ground.
10. **"Sozin's Comet, Part 2: The Old Masters"** (3.19) and **"Part 3: Into the Inferno"** (3.20). Bumi's pillar launch
    at the liberation of Ba Sing Se (wiki); Toph's metalbending at Wulong Forest; Aang's rock armour vs Ozai. Look at: a pillar launching a
    body; sheets of metal folding.
11. **"Sozin's Comet, Part 4: Avatar Aang"** (3.21). Aang's elemental sphere of orbiting rocks, slab shackles,
    compressed rock bullets. Look at: stones orbiting a body; shackle slabs closing.
12. **Korra: "Venom of the Red Lotus"** (Book Three, 13) and **"The Battle of Zaofu"** (4.6). Lin and Su trap Zaheer
    with slab shackles; Kuvira traps Korra; metalbending cables. Look at: hand-drawn slabs closing round wrists.

## 7. Mapping to our earth moves (`data/sway/moves.json` labels)

| Ours | Show shape to study | Notes for the gather |
|---|---|---|
| Pebbles | small rocks pulled up and thrown | stance drop, a fist-sized rock pulled from the deck's edge, held a beat, thrown with a punch |
| Pillar | rock column, earth launch | upward fist or kick; 4-6 frame rise with a dust burst; sinks back or crumbles |
| Earth Wall | earth wall, rock shield | two-arm rise, ragged top, strata lines, dust on the rise |
| Fissure | fissure, earthquake | stomp, crack runs along the deck, dust along the line, scar stays |
| Stone Form | earth armour, gauntlet, rock gloves | rocks fly from the deck to fists and shoulders; crumble away on release |
| Burrow | earth tunneling, earth wave | ground heaves as a ridge; the body sinks into it |
| Earth Grip | slab shackles, rock cuffs, sinking | triangular slabs close on wrists or ankles |
| Catch | stopping a thrown rock | rock slows into the palm, dust falls off |
| Patch, Mend | re-forming earth | cracks close, rubble flows back |

The deck is stone and earth over water: the gather should visibly take material from the deck's edge or floor (a
crack, a lifted slab, grit falling away from its underside) and the leftover should be rubble on the deck, not
nothing.
