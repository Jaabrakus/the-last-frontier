## 1. The art behind it

- Water is Yang and Chen style taijiquan (tai chi): slow arcs, the waist turns first, the arms follow in circles, the
  feet step in curves. "Push and pull" is the principle (the Moon and Ocean spirits are Tui, push, and La, pull).
  Defence becomes offence: a stream is caught, swung round the body and sent back. (Wiki: Connection, Fighting style,
  Spirituality.)
- A waterbender needs a source; travelling benders carry a water skin; masters pull water from plants, air, sweat.
  Power is higher at night and at the full moon; heat weakens it. (Wiki: Weaknesses, Weapons.)
- Styles: Northern (defensive, architectural, ice), Southern (more aggressive), Foggy Swamp (stiff upright stance,
  arms turning like a propeller), pro-bending (short blasts and blocks, boxer-like punches, high kicks to direct
  streams). (Wiki: Styles.) Our ring is closest to pro-bending in pace, but the gather should read as the older art.

## 2. How water is gathered

- **From a river, lake or sea** (memory). Palms open towards the water; one hand lifts in an arc and the water
  follows as a ribbon about a forearm thick with a rounded tip, lagging the hand by a few frames and bending where
  the hand turned. The other hand takes it over and it travels round the body in figure-eights. The wiki names this
  "stream the water": "a move that draws water from a source that waterbenders move around their bodies"; it is the
  first training move and used by every level (Katara; Pakku idles with it in "The Waterbending Master").
- **From a water skin.** The stopper pops and a thin stream snakes along the arm and thickens in the air. A skin's
  worth makes a thin whip or a small shield; big moves need a real source. (Wiki: Weapons: Katara carries one.)
- **From puddles, wet ground, snow** (memory). The surface swells into a mound and a tendril lifts off it; snow lifts
  as a block that softens in the hand.
- **From plants and air.** Hama draws water out of fire lilies and trees ("The Puppetmaster"): the plants wither as
  the water leaves them. Condensing water from the air gives only a small amount, enough for ice claws. (Wiki:
  Plantbending, Condensation.)
- **From the body.** Sweat, saliva, breath (ice breath), rain held in the air (Katara over Yon Rha in "The Southern
  Raiders").
- **Timing of the gather** (typical): a one-hand lift from a river to shoulder height about 0.5-0.8 s (12-20 frames);
  an arc over the head about 1 s. The water stays 2-4 frames behind the hand and overshoots when the hand stops.
- **The body during the gather:** waist leads, arms follow in slow circles, weight shifts foot to foot in curves. Big
  moves use both arms in one arc; the wave is a two-arm lift and then a push.

## 3. The shapes of the classic moves

Each entry: the gesture, the drawn shape, gather, release, what the water does at the end. "Ours" is the game's move
the shape maps to (section 7).

- **Water whip** (ours: Lash). The wiki: "a lashing tendril of water to swipe at an opponent; the shape, size, and
  length are all determined by the bender's control"; it can be sharpened into a blade that slices metal. Gather a
  ribbon, swing it overhead or behind, snap the arm forward. The body is a tapered tube; on the snap the tip
  stretches thin for 1-2 frames (smear) and cracks at the far end with a small spray star (memory). Gather 0.5-1 s,
  release 2-4 frames; the tendril then falls to a splash or is drawn into the next sweep. Taught in "The
  Waterbending Scroll". Masters raise four or five at once (Katara vs Azula, "Sozin's Comet, Part 4").
- **Stream / water jet / water bullet** (ours: Splash). Jet: a high-pressure tube that can blast through a target
  (Aang vs a pirate; Katara on the Southern Raiders ship). Bullet: "basic yet useful because it takes little time to
  perform" (Hama vs Katara). Both end in a crown splash: a ring of white spikes and a wet sheen on the ground
  (memory). Water kick: the stream sent with a high kick (Korra, pro-bending).
- **Octopus form.** A body of water formed round the user into multiple whip-like tendrils that grasp, strike or
  intercept. The wiki: first shown by Aang during his training with Katara ("The Cave of Two Lovers"), used by Katara
  against the Dai Li ("The Crossroads of Destiny"); Ming-Hua (Korra) grows the arms straight from her body and
  freezes only the tips. Six to eight tapered tubes with rounded tips, each tracking independently (memory).
- **Wave** (ours: Wave). "By moving a large mass of water without separating it from its original source ... an
  upward movement that raises the source, which they subsequently send away." A hump rolls at the target, breaks with
  a white crest and ends as a sheet sliding over the ground (memory). Pakku in his duel; Aang in the Avatar State.
- **Water shield / wall / ice wall** (ours: Ice Wall). A sheet lifted in front, cupped like a palm. Frozen it becomes
  a pale slab with faceted straight edges; the freeze crawls across as a white crackle in 2-4 frames (memory). The
  wiki warns a hastily raised shield "needs some type of compression or else it will not be effective" (Katara vs
  Zuko at the Spirit Oasis).
- **Ice spear, iceberg spike, ice disc** (ours: Spear). A stream freezes into a spike and is fired (Pakku vs Katara;
  Katara over Yon Rha); spikes rise from the water as angular pale-blue columns (the Northern Tribe's defence, Hama's
  iceberg spike under a ship); discs are sliced off an ice column (Katara vs Pakku). Ice is drawn with hard straight
  lines and white edges, the opposite of water's curves; it shatters into pale chips.
- **Ice blade, ice claws, ice gauntlet** (ours: Ice Tomahawk). Water drawn round the hand or fingers and frozen into
  a blade or claws (Hama; Ming-Hua; the wiki notes ice claws use "little water and can be performed quickly").
- **Creeping ice, slip over ice** (ours: Frost). A ray of ice runs along the ground from the bender to the target and
  freezes their feet (Katara's early accidents; Pakku at the siege), or a floor of ice to slide on and trip others.
- **Water gliding, ice sled, water spout** (ours: Skate). An ice board under the feet (Katara vs the serpent in "The
  Serpent's Pass"); a sled; the spout, a spinning column that lifts the bender, "a giant snake of water around the
  lower body" (Pakku vs firebenders in "The Siege of the North, Part 2").
- **Redirection** (ours: Turn). Catch the opponent's stream, swing it round and send it back, often frozen (Katara
  vs Sangok; Korra vs Viper in "Welcome to Republic City"). The caught water visibly reverses its curve (memory).
- **Healing** (ours: Patch, Mend). Water laid over the wound like a glove, glowing pale; hands hover and circle.
- **Phase change.** Mist and steam for cover; ice breath. "The mark of a master ... swiftly change from one state of
  water to another": Pakku deflects a stream, freezes it and slides on it behind Katara.
- **Rarer:** bubble, water cloak, water drill, maelstrom, suspending rain, bloodbending: see the wiki page.

## 4. Timing at a glance (typical 2D figures)

| Move | Gather | Release | Travel | End |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whip | 0.5-1 s, ribbon trailing the arm | 2-4 frames, tip smeared | fast, curved | crack star, then falls |
| Bullet | 0.2-0.4 s | 2 frames | straight | crown splash |
| Wave | 0.8-1.5 s, both arms rising | 4-6 frames | a rolling hump | sheet slides out |
| Ice shield | 0.3-0.5 s lift | freeze crawl 2-4 frames | holds | shatters or melts |
| Ice spear | 0.4-0.8 s | freeze 2-3 frames, thrown 2 frames | straight | shatters into chips |
| Octopus | 1-1.5 s to raise the ring | each arm 2-3 frames | arms track independently | collapses as a splash |

## 5. Reference moments (episode pages are `https://avatar.fandom.com/wiki/<Title_With_Underscores>`)

1. **"The Waterbending Scroll"** (1.9). Katara learns the whip from the scroll on a riverbank. Look at: the ribbon
   trailing the hand on the lift, the tip's snap, failure drawn as water simply falling.
2. **"The Waterbending Master"** (1.18). Katara vs Pakku: his wave, ice discs from a column, ice spikes, Katara
   anchoring her feet in ice. Look at: a master switching liquid to ice mid-move; the facets.
3. **"The Siege of the North, Part 1"** (1.19). Katara vs Zuko at the Spirit Oasis: night whips, the shield that
   fails, the ice dome. Look at: the silver night palette; the freeze crawl.
4. **"The Siege of the North, Part 2"** (1.20). Pakku's spout and creeping ice against firebenders; Aang's Avatar
   State wave. Look at: the spout coiling round the legs; a wave at fleet scale.
5. **"The Cave of Two Lovers"** (2.2). The wiki's first octopus form (Aang training with Katara). Look at: the ring
   round the waist rising into tendrils.
6. **"The Serpent's Pass"** (2.12). The air bubble over the lake bottom; Katara's ice surfboard. Look at: water as a
   transparent dome; the board's spray.
7. **"The Drill"** (2.13). Water pressed to a razor edge "for a split-second" to cut the drill's braces, and mud
   bent as thick water. Look at: the one-frame edge.
8. **"The Crossroads of Destiny"** (2.20). Katara's octopus form vs the Dai Li; water cloak vs Azula in the Crystal
   Catacombs; water whips vs Zuko's fire whips. Look at: tentacles tracking independently; water meeting fire.
9. **"The Puppetmaster"** (3.8). Hama draws water from fire lilies and the air; the full-moon fight. Look at: beads
   lifting from plants; the water ring round the body.
10. **"The Southern Raiders"** (3.16). Katara stops the rain over Yon Rha and freezes it into spears. Look at:
    suspended droplets with white highlights; spear tips.
11. **"Sozin's Comet, Part 4: Avatar Aang"** (3.21). Katara's multiple whips and the ice prison on Azula. Look at:
    four-five whips at once; the prison's facets.
12. **Korra: "Welcome to Republic City"** (Book One, 1), **"A Leaf in the Wind"** (1.2), **"And the Winner Is..."**
    (1.6). Ice wall vs a metalbending officer; catching Viper's stream and freezing it round his head; lashes,
    bullets and kicks in the ring. Look at: fast boxed releases with short gathers.
13. **Korra: "The Ultimatum"** (Book Three, 11), **"Venom of the Red Lotus"** (3.13). Ming-Hua's water arms vs Kya,
    then Mako. Look at: water as a limb, locally frozen tips.

water round the deck (or from a skin at the hip for small moves) and travel up to the arms before any cast.
