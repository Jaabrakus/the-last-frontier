# Hidden gems and folklore: research report for Two Fires and later storylines

I did web research and read `docs/main_story.md` and `docs/maps.md`. I edited no files, ran no Godot or build process, and ran no heavy local command. The session's web-search budget ran out near the end, so the items marked "unverified" below still need a check.

Certainty marks: **[High]** means several solid sources or archaeology agree. **[Med]** means one good source, or the details vary between sources. **[Low]** means legend, family tradition, or a source written long after the event.

## Summary

1. **The Lake George and Champlain corridor is the strongest new map.** About half the best events below happened there: the sunken fleet, Sabbath Day Point, both Battles on Snowshoes, Fort William Henry, Inverawe's ghost and Bloody Pond. It is a lake between two hostile forts, which already has the shape of an Arc Raiders map. You go out from a fort, cross contested ground, take loot or prisoners and get back. It also works with the consultation slots empty: the enemies it needs are French regulars, Canadian militia, deserters and smugglers.
2. **Several real events mirror The Ford.** The 1766 Prendergast tenant rising, the Green Mountain Boys against the "Yorkers" (1770–74), and John Hendricks Lydius, a real Albany smuggler with doubtful land deeds, all echo The Ford's land fight. Lydius is a ready-made historical model for Silas Vane.
3. **Colonial folklore can support an optional supernatural layer that never touches Native beliefs.** The best-documented pieces are: Pennsylvania German braucherei, the witch-masters and silver bullets that Doddridge recorded, a real 1740s Pennsylvania witch bottle, German will-o'-the-wisp tales, treasure-guarding spirits, the Moravian Lot, and the Shakers with the Dark Day of 1780. One German tale fits The Ford exactly: dishonest land surveyors are doomed to walk as will-o'-the-wisps.
4. **Watch the dates.** Much "Hudson Valley folklore" was written in the 19th century: Irving in 1819–22, Whittier in 1867, Skinner in 1896. It works as mood, but a character in 1757 should not quote it.

## A. True events (1750–1780)

### Lake George, Champlain and the Hudson corridor

- **The sunken fleet, autumn 1758.** After the defeat at Carillon, the British deliberately sank the radeau *Land Tortoise*, the sloop *Halifax* and 260 bateaux in Lake George to hide them from French raiders over the winter. They meant to raise them in spring. Most were raised in 1759. The *Land Tortoise*, a floating battery 52 ft long built for seven cannon, was never recovered. Sonar found it in 1990 in 105 ft of water. **[High]** https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=17384 , https://www.archaeologicalconservancy.org/bookreviews/the-lost-radeau-north-americas-oldest-intact-warship/
- **Sabbath Day Point, 23 July 1757.** Col. John Parker's New Jersey Blues, about 350 men in 22 whaleboats, went out from Fort William Henry to burn a French sawmill. They were ambushed by about 450 French and allied warriors under Ensign de Corbière. Parker lost about 250 men, roughly 160 killed or drowned and the rest taken. **[High]** for the event, **[Med]** for the numbers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sabbath_Day_Point , https://emergingrevolutionarywar.org/2019/11/13/slaughter-at-sabbath-day-point/
- **The two Battles on Snowshoes.**
  - The first was on 21 January 1757 near Carillon. Rogers was wounded and the rangers retreated with heavy losses. **[High]** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/rogers_robert_4E.html , https://fortticonderoga.org/events/fort-events/1757-battle-on-snowshoes-battle-re-enactment/detail
  - The second was on 13 March 1758 on the west side of Lake George, a heavy defeat for Rogers. It gave rise to the legend that he escaped by sliding about 400 ft down "Rogers Rock" onto the ice. The French believed he was dead because he left behind his coat with his commission in it. **[High]** for the battle, **[Low]** for the slide. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_on_Snowshoes
- **The rangers were a mixed corps.** Fort Ticonderoga records English, Irish, Scottish, Welsh, German, Portuguese and Dutch men. A Black ranger, Jacob Jones, "was in Rogers Rangers three Years" and was freed after his service. There were also Stockbridge Mohican and Mohegan companies, which fall under the consultation rule. **[Med–High]** https://fortticonderoga.org/events/fort-events/1757-battle-on-snowshoes-battle-re-enactment/detail
- **Robert Rogers was a counterfeiter first.** In 1754 he was indicted with a counterfeiting gang, and the case "never came to trial." **[High]** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/rogers_robert_4E.html
  - He raised a company in 1755, and the case lapsed. His colonel, Joseph Blanchard, was the justice on his case. His lieutenant, John Stark, had testified against him. **[Med]** https://emergingrevolutionarywar.org/tag/robert-rogers/
  - He was tried for treason in 1768 and acquitted, under "great reason to suspect." **[High]** (same DCB entry)
- **Owen Sullivan's "Dover Money Club."** Sullivan was a Boston silversmith turned forger who ran the colonies' most elaborate counterfeiting ring from remote Dutchess County on the New York–Connecticut border. He claimed over £25,000 in forged bills. Connecticut caught him and sent him to New York, where he was hanged on 10 May 1756. In his gallows appeal he urged his 29 confederates to quit the trade. **[High]** for the hanging, **[Med]** for the details. https://www.executedtoday.com/2016/05/10/ , https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2011/11/10/american-dream-money/ , https://coinbooks.org/esylum_v14n06a09.html
- **Fort William Henry, August 1757, and after.**
  - Rumour at the time said 1,500 or more were killed. Most modern estimates put the dead at no more than 184, and some go as low as about 50. **[High]** for the event, **[Med]** for the numbers. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_William_Henry , https://historynet.com/fort-william-henry-1757-a-fate-worse-than-surrender/
  - Many captives were ransomed.
  - Allied warriors had come a very long way expecting captives and plunder, and felt cheated by the European surrender terms.
  - Smallpox taken from the sick and the dead spread to their home communities, which makes this Native-side content that needs consultation.
- **Bloody Pond, 8 September 1755.** This was the third fight in the Battle of Lake George. Tradition says the dead were rolled into the pond and turned it red. **[High]** for the fight, **[Med]** for the pond tradition. https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/forts/forts-d-index/bloody-pond-battle
- **Smuggling on the Hudson–Champlain–Richelieu route, about 1716–1754.** Montreal furs went south for English woollens "under the noses of authorities." Kahnawake was a hub, and the carriers were mainly Mohawk women, which falls under the consultation rule. The Desauniers sisters ran a trade at Kahnawake from 1726. Albany's Robert Sanders encouraged the trade. **[High]** for the trade, **[Med]** for the details; the sisters' names and dates are still unverified. https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/fortchambly/activ/expositions-exhibitions/robert-sanders , https://pbswisconsin.org/watch/university-place/university-place-women-smugglers-early-america-1701-1754/ , https://oieahc.wm.edu/?p=3016
- **John Hendricks Lydius (1704–1791), a real-life Silas Vane.** **[High]** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/lydius_john_hendricks_4E.html
  - He was an Albany smuggler. New France fined him 3,000 livres and banished him on 28 September 1730.
  - He settled at the Hudson carrying place, later Fort Edward, a post "astride the trade route" that "annoyed many."
  - In 1754 he bought Wyoming Valley land by dealing with six sachems separately instead of in council. An Oneida denounced him as a man who "stole our lands," and William Johnson called him "a Man extreamly obnoxious to the public."
- **Jane McCrea, 27 July 1777, near Fort Edward.** She was killed while trying to reach her Loyalist fiancé in Burgoyne's army. The published Gates–Burgoyne letters turned her death into propaganda that brought men out to the militia. **[High]** for the event, **[Med]** for who killed her and how. https://home.nps.gov/sara/learn/historyculture/jane-mccrea.htm , https://teachingamericanhistory.org/document/generals-gates-and-burgoyne-on-the-murder-of-jane-mccrea/

### The Mohawk Valley (our existing map)

- **Fort Bull at the Oneida Carry, 27 March 1756.** Chaussegros de Léry's force destroyed the fort, and its stores were blown up or dumped into Wood Creek. Binghamton University is surveying the battlefield. This is the valley's north-west exit in maps.md. **[High]** https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/early-military-history-of-the-oneida-carry.htm , https://www.binghamton.edu/programs/public-archaeology-facility/projects/fortbull/
- **German Flatts, 12 November 1757, verifying main_story.md.** **[High]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_German_Flatts_(1757) , https://museum.dmna.ny.gov/forts/burnets-field
  - About 300 men under Picoté de Belestre attacked about 75 militia at around 3 a.m.
  - 40–50 people were killed or drowned and more than 150 taken. Sources disagree on houses burned: one figure is 60, another says all buildings.
  - Friendly Oneida had warned the settlers, who made no defensive preparations.
  - The 42nd Highlanders arrived to find ruins. **[Med]**
  - A second attack came in April 1758, and Nicholas Herkimer repelled it. **[Med]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Herkimer
- **The Palatines' own back-story.**
  - Germans who came in 1710 were bound to burn tar for naval stores in camps on the Hudson near Livingston Manor before they reached Schoharie. **[High]**
  - Conrad Weiser lived with the Mohawk over the winter and spring of 1712–13 as a teenager and became Pennsylvania's chief interpreter. He is a real Palatine "between two worlds" like the player. **[High]** https://conradweiserhomestead.org/timeline/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Weiser
  - Weiser joined the Ephrata mystic cloister for a time as "Brother Enoch." **[Med]** https://www.SelfDefinition.org/christian/Ephrata-Coister-Historical-Account-by-A.M.Aurand-1940.pdf
- **Slavery in the valley.** Sir William Johnson enslaved about 60 Africans and was one of the province's largest slaveholders. Johnson Hall's restored stonehouse now interprets their lives. **[High]** https://parks.ny.gov/historic-sites/10/details.aspx , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnstown,_New_York

### Hudson tenant country and the New Hampshire Grants

- **The Prendergast rising, 1765–66.** **[High]** https://history.nycourts.gov/case/crown-v-prendergast/
  - The Philipse family won its claim against the Wappinger in 1765 and imposed manorial leases. The Wappinger claim was led by Daniel Nimham and needs a consultation slot.
  - About 1,700 armed tenant "levellers" rose. The 28th Regiment was sent against them on 20 June 1766.
  - William Prendergast was tried for high treason on 6 August 1766 and sentenced to be "hung, drawn and quartered."
  - His Quaker wife, Mehitable Wing, helped his defence so effectively that the prosecution tried to have her removed, saying "she might too much influence the Jury."
  - He was reprieved on 1 September 1766 and later received a royal pardon.
  - The story of her "daring horseback ride" for the pardon comes from a descendant institution. **[Low–Med]** https://www.prendergastlibrary.org/?p=5123
- **The Green Mountain Boys, 1770–74.** **[High]** for the movement. https://allthingsliberty.com/2013/03/the-legacy-of-ethan-allen/ , https://vermonthistory.org/freedom-unity-green-mountain-boys , https://britannica.com/print/article/245031
  - New Hampshire grantees resisted New York ("Yorker") title.
  - They whipped surveyors, burned cabins, and hoisted a Yorker in a chair up the Catamount Tavern's sign.
  - Their whipping punishment was the "beech seal," a beating with beech rods. **[Med]** https://www.wehd.com/thornton/Beech_seal.html
  - In 1774 New York made Allen and others subject to death, and Allen answered with his own bounties.
- **Claudius Smith, "Cowboy of the Ramapos."** He was a Loyalist outlaw who stole livestock and ambushed travellers from a cave den, and was hanged at Goshen on 22 January 1779. **[High]** Legend says he buried his plunder in the hills. **[Low]** https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/claudius-smith-cowboy-of-the-ramapos-hangs , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius_Smith

### Pennsylvania and the Ohio country

- **Regina Leininger and the hymn at Carlisle.**
  - She was taken at Penn's Creek on 16 October 1755. Her father and brother were killed. **[Med–High]**
  - On 31 December 1764, among the captives Bouquet brought back, her mother could not recognise her. Bouquet suggested something from the past, and the mother sang the hymn "Allein und doch nicht ganz allein." The daughter joined in. **[Med]**
  - The source is Muhlenberg, but he never gave her surname, and her identity was argued later. https://www.chasingfamilyghosts.com/post/a-curious-letter-part-four-facts-v-legend , https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=15796
- **Mary Draper Ingles, 1755.** Shawnee warriors took her from Draper's Meadows to the Ohio. She escaped with an "old Dutch woman," meaning a German, and walked about 500 miles home along the rivers in about 40 days. The two separated after a fight brought on by hunger. **[High]** The Shawnee side needs consultation. https://www.nps.gov/neri/learn/historyculture/mary-draper-ingles.htm
- **Christian Frederick Post, 1758.** A Moravian lay missionary, he went with the Delaware leader Pisquetomen to the Kuskusky towns from August to November 1758. Those councils detached the Ohio nations from the French and let Forbes take Fort Duquesne unopposed. **[High]** It is the rare "diplomacy, not war" mission, and it needs consultation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Frederick_Post
- **Provincial soldiers who quit on their contract.** At Louisbourg, from September to November 1759, Massachusetts men whose terms had expired refused all duty and petitioned to go home. The mutiny was forgiven, and 140 men sailed for Boston. **[High]** https://www.massmoments.org/moment-details/massachusetts-soldiers-threaten-mutiny.html

### Women's roles and Black lives (beyond the above)

- **Camp followers.** They cooked, did laundry, sewed and nursed. They were paid and fed for it, and a company might have up to about ten. **[Med]** https://allthingsliberty.com/2014/04/8-fast-facts-about-camp-followers/ , https://wams.nyhistory.org/colonies-and-revolution/english-colonies/women-of-the-french-and-indian-war/
- **Susanna Johnson, 1754.** She was captured at Charlestown No. 4 and gave birth on the march to a daughter she named Captive. The family was sold to the French and held in prison in Quebec. **[High]** Her captors were Abenaki, so this needs consultation. https://newenglandhistoricalsociety.com/susannah-johnson-indian-captive-gives-birth-wild-commemorates-spot-45-years-later/ , https://www.morrin.org/en/prisonniers-eleves-et-penseurs-susannah-willard-johnson/
- **Lucy Terry Prince.**
  - Her "Bars Fight" (1746) is the earliest known poem by an African American. **[High]**
  - She married the free Black man Abijah Prince in 1756, and they moved to Guilford, Vermont. The year is 1764 in one source and 1775 in the other. **[Med]**
  - She later argued her own land case. The Deerfield museum says the Vermont Supreme Court; the popular "U.S. Supreme Court" version is doubtful. **[Low]** https://aane.deerfield-ma.org/people/aaperson/69/ , https://britannica.com/print/article/588418
- **Colonel Tye (about 1753–1780).** He escaped slavery in Monmouth County, New Jersey in 1775. He led the Loyalist "Black Brigade" on night raids that freed enslaved people and took cattle, and died of tetanus in 1780. **[High]** https://www.blackpast.org/african-american-history/colonel-tye-1753-1780/
- **Fleeing north was not freedom.** New France held more than 1,000 enslaved Black people by 1759. **[High]** https://ville.montreal.qc.ca/memoiresdesmontrealais/des-esclaves-montreal
- **Pinkster in Albany.** This was a Dutch Pentecost festival that enslaved Africans made their own, on Pinkster Hill under a "King Charles." The Common Council banned it in 1811. Its documentation is mostly from the 1790s to 1811, so it fits Albany in later storylines. **[High]** for the festival and the ban, **[Med]** for King Charles. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinkster , https://nysm.nysed.gov/node/34627

## B. Folklore the colonists themselves held (fair to use)

### Pennsylvania German and Palatine

- **Braucherei ("powwow").** This is folk healing and protective charms, of European origin, brought over by German-speaking migrants in the 17th and 18th centuries. Hohman's *Der lange verborgene Freund* is later (1820), and the name "Pow-Wows" was added only in late 19th-century reprints. Use "Brauche" or "Braucherei" in game, not "powwow." **[High]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_Hohman , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braucherei
- **The Himmelsbrief ("heaven's letter").** A protective letter claimed to come from God, carried folded in soldiers' uniforms "in all of America's wars," and in Pennsylvania part of powwow practice. **[Med]**; the earliest American printing date is unverified. https://librarycompany.org/broadsides/section3b.htm , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himmelsbrief
- **Will-o'-the-wisps (Irrlichter).** German tales say they are the souls of unbaptised children, or of sinners who lead travellers into marshes. One tale in Grimm's *Deutsche Sagen* (1816), "The Cursed Land Surveyors," condemns dishonest surveyors to wander after death; Bechstein (1853) names boundary-stone movers among the sinners. **[High]** that these tales exist. That Mohawk Valley Palatines told this exact tale is my inference. **[Low–Med]** https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/willowisp.html
- **The Elbedritsch.** A made-up bird-like creature from the Palatinate whose "hunt" is a snipe-hunt prank: the newcomer holds the sack and the lantern while everyone else slips off to the tavern. The belief reportedly came to Pennsylvania with 18th-century emigrants. **[Med]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elwetritsch , https://www.padutchdictionary.com/entries/nouns/Elbedritsch.html
- **The Palatine Light.** The *Princess Augusta* (about 340 Palatine emigrants, only 68 surviving the crossing) was wrecked at Block Island on 27 December 1738. Rumours of mistreatment grew into a burning ghost-ship legend. **[High]** for the wreck; the legend was shaped in the 19th century, including Whittier's 1867 poem. **[Low]** as an 18th-century belief. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_Light , https://masshist.org/beehiveblog/2018/03/book-review-the-palatine-wreck-the-legend-of-the-new-england-ghost-ship

### Backcountry witch belief (Scots-Irish, English, German)

- **Doddridge's witchcraft chapter.** Joseph Doddridge's *Notes on the Settlement and Indian Wars… 1763 to 1783* (1824), written by a man raised on that frontier, says settlers believed:
  - witches caused strange diseases in children,
  - they killed cattle with "hair balls,"
  - they bewitched guns,
  - they rode men as horses.
  - "Witch masters" countered them by drawing the witch's picture on a board and shooting it with a bullet containing silver.

  **[High]** that he recorded this. https://archive.org/download/PDFArchive_201901/notesonsettlemen00doddrich.pdf , https://www.heraldstaronline.com/opinion/local-columns/2023/10/history-in-the-hills-witches-of-cross-creek/

  A Cross Creek case from about 1810 ended with the accused old woman dead. **[Low–Med]**
- **The Essington witch bottle, Pennsylvania, about 1748.** It was buried upside down by the house, held six pins and probably urine, and was meant to turn a witch's harm back on her. It was apparently planted by a Quaker family. **[High]**, from archaeology. https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/halloween/witch_bottle.html
- **Margaret Mattson, 1684.** With Penn presiding, the jury found her guilty only of having "the common fame of a witch." It is an early, very Pennsylvanian answer to witch-panic. **[High]** https://www.jenkinslaw.org/news/witch-trial-margaret-matson
- **Catskill and Schoharie witch doctors.** Alf Evers' *The Catskills* (1972) records post-Revolution witch doctors such as "Dr." Jacob Brink, who chased witches out of butter churns and stopped bleeding from miles away. The Greene County Historian's summary was hosted at theschoharienews.com/2021/11, which now redirects to an unrelated site, so cite the book itself. **[Low–Med]**
- **Moll Derry.** A late-1700s Fayette County "hex doctor" who reportedly cursed three mockers to hang. **[Low]**, local legend. https://www.observer-reporter.com/living/2019/nov/05/witches-next-door-greene-county-history-thick-with-sorcery/

### Dutch Hudson and the Catskills

- **The Heer of the Dunderberg.** A Dutch goblin who commands storms at the Highlands gate, with Dutch skippers lowering their peaks to him. The source is Irving's "The Storm-Ship" (1822), retold by Skinner in 1896. **[Low]** as an attested 18th-century custom. https://sacred-texts.com/ame/lol/lol013.htm
- **Rip Van Winkle (1819).** Irving largely borrowed it from Otmar's German tale "Peter Klaus" (1800): a goatherd drinks with strange players in the mountains and sleeps for twenty years. So the "Catskill" sleeper is really a German motif, which suits a Palatine teller. **[High]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Klaus
- **Sleepy Hollow (1820).** It is set in 1790 and its horseman is a Hessian. It is fiction, and the "real decapitated Hessian" stories are hearsay. **[Low]** Use it only for post-1777 storylines.
- **Captain Kidd and the money-diggers.**
  - Treasure was believed to be guarded by ghosts or goblins, and to slip away if handled "disrespectfully."
  - Diggers used peep stones, hazel or mineral rods, and pumps to keep the water out.
  - An 1823 Albany paper describes a Kidd hill south of Albany as a rendezvous of "infernal spirits."
  - Alan Taylor calls this the region's "supernatural economy" of 1780–1830.

  **[Med]** https://www.dialoguejournal.com/articles/joseph-smith-captain-kidd-lore-and-treasure-seeking-in-new-york-and-new-england-during-the-early-republic/ , https://vtdigger.org/2017/10/29/gold-hidden-hills-thought/

### Wider colonial lore, visions and portents

- **The Leeds Devil, "1735."** Mother Leeds' thirteenth child, cursed at birth. The Leeds family did live at Leeds Point. Brian Regal traces the "monster" to Quaker hostility toward Daniel Leeds' astrological almanac and to Franklin's rivalry with Titan Leeds. It is set in the New Jersey Pine Barrens, so use it as a travellers' tale or almanac satire. **[Med]** https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/history-magazine/article/the-devil-went-down-to-new-jersey , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Devil
- **Haunted battlefields.**
  - **Inverawe:** Duncan Campbell of the 42nd died of wounds from Carillon (18 July 1758). Legend says his murdered cousin's ghost promised to meet him "at Ticonderoga," a name he had never heard. Stevenson's 1887 poem fixed the story. **[High]** for the death, **[Low]** for the ghost. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Campbell_(died_1758) , https://www.worldhistory.org/article/2815/the-legend-of-ticonderoga/
  - **Bloody Pond:** see section A.
- **The Moravian Lot.** Bethlehem's Moravians put marriages, membership and offices to the Lot after prayer. There were three slips: yes, no, and a blank meaning "wait." **[High]** https://journey.salem.edu/history-blog/decision-by-lot/ , https://historicbethlehem.org/about-us/blog-post/moravian-love-and-marriage-practices
- **Ephrata Cloister.** Founded by Conrad Beissel in 1732, a celibate mystic community of about 300 at its peak around 1750. **[High]** https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/d4e1fd44-3739-441c-bbf8-41920efebfc1
- **The Shakers and the Dark Day.** **[High]** https://www.history.com/articles/who-is-mother-ann-lee-shakers-founder-real , https://www.britannica.com/biography/Ann-Lee
  - Ann Lee's group settled at Niskayuna near Albany in 1776. Neighbours suspected her of witchcraft or spying, and she was jailed for preaching against fighting.
  - On the Dark Day, 19 May 1780, wildfire smoke blacked out the noon sun, and many converts followed.
- **The Public Universal Friend.** Jemima Wilkinson "died" in October 1776 and rose as a genderless spirit, and later founded Jerusalem, New York (1794). **[High]**; a 1790s hook. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Universal_Friend
- **The Cape Ann earthquake, 18 November 1755.** Felt from Nova Scotia to South Carolina 17 days after Lisbon, it set off sermons and verses about God's warning. **[High]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1755_Cape_Ann_earthquake , https://www.masshist.org/database/494
- **A myth to avoid: Champlain seeing "Champ" in 1609.** The quote was invented for *Vermont Life* in 1970; what Champlain actually described was a gar. **[High]** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champ_(folklore)

## C. What not to use: Native sacred beings

Do not use the wendigo, skinwalkers, or any other Native spiritual being, healer, prophet or ceremony as magic, monsters or loot. The reasons:

1. **They are living beliefs, not dead folklore.** When J.K. Rowling used skinwalkers in 2016, the Cherokee scholar Adrienne Keene objected that "Native spirituality and religions are not fantasy on the same level as wizards. These beliefs are alive, practiced, and protected." https://getreligion.org/getreligion/2016/3/11/native-americans-are-thumbs-down-on-jk-rowling , https://historymatters.sites.sheffield.ac.uk/blog-archive/2016/jk-rowlings-ilvermorny-a-school-in-need-of-a-history-lesson
2. **The wendigo is already a well-known example of appropriation.** Anishinaabe and Cree stories have been turned into generic horror monsters (for example in *Until Dawn*) that cast Indigeneity as the threat. Anishinaabe authors such as Waubgeshig Rice are reclaiming it. https://reinventionjournal.org/index.php/reinvention/article/view/906 , https://canlitguides.ca/gage-karahkwiio-diabo/the-futures-of-indigenous-horror-moon-of-the-crusted-snow/research-activities-and-resources/
3. **Skinwalkers are Navajo and not even from this region.** Using them would also break the project's rule that Native content waits for a nation's sign-off.

**Related cases to keep inside the consultation slot:**
- the "Indian burial ground" and "Indian curse" tropes;
- Champ's link to Abenaki stories;
- the Saint-Francis "silver Madonna" treasure (below);
- the Wappinger claim in Prendergast;
- the smallpox and ransom content at Fort William Henry;
- Kahnawake smuggling carriers;
- Stockbridge rangers;
- Shawnee and Delaware towns in the Ingles and Post stories.

The rule for the supernatural layer: **it only voices what colonists believed about themselves, their neighbours and their God.**

## D. Mission and storyline hooks

These are designed to work with consultation slots empty. Each lists the history, what you play, the features it needs, and how it would work with the optional folklore layer on.

**1. The Sunken Fleet (Lake George, autumn 1758 to spring 1759). This is the PvPvE showcase.**
- **History:** 260 bateaux, the *Halifax* and the *Land Tortoise* were scuttled to hide them from raiders. https://www.hmdb.org/m.asp?m=17384
- **Play:**
  - Autumn: sink the boats at night before French scouts find them. This is stealth, and it uses boats.
  - Spring: "raising days." Co-op teams drag up bateaux with ropes and oxen. Rivals try to steal them for the French: deserters, smugglers, or other players in PvP.
  - The *Land Tortoise* is the rumoured rare wreck, worth the most and the hardest to find.
- **Arc Raiders analogue:** the fort is the hub, the lake is topside, and night and ice are "special conditions" with higher rewards. https://arcraiders.com/news/everything-you-need-to-know
- **Needs:** drag, winch and rope work on boats; ice.
- **Folklore layer:** marsh lights over the wrecks.

**2. To Counterfeit (Dutchess County / Connecticut line, 1754–56). A lead-in to a Rangers storyline.**
- **History:** Sullivan's Dover Money Club and Rogers' indictment. https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2011/11/10/american-dream-money/ , https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/rogers_robert_4E.html
- **Play:**
  - Pass forged bills at taverns and the sutler's, where suspicion builds into a hue and cry.
  - Guard the engraver's swamp camp.
  - Then choose: testify (as Stark did), hang, or enlist for the Crown Point expedition and have the case quietly dropped.
- **Needs:** a money item with a "forged" flag, a shop suspicion check, and the court flow from The Ford's Magistrate mission.
- **PvP:** engraved plates as high-value loot.

**3. Snowshoes (Lake George, January 1757 / March 1758)**
- **History:** both Battles on Snowshoes, and the Slide legend. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_on_Snowshoes
- **Play:**
  - A winter scout with ranger rules: march in single file, double back, split up when attacked. The battle is a fighting retreat.
  - A companion such as Jacob Jones, a Black ranger serving for his freedom, can carry a personal side chain. https://fortticonderoga.org/events/fort-events/1757-battle-on-snowshoes-battle-re-enactment/detail
- **Folklore layer:** the 400 ft slide becomes a real, terrifying sled-down-the-cliff escape. In history mode, you find the coat Rogers left behind and get a choice: plant it so the French think he died, or not.
- **Needs:** snowshoes (already planned), ice, cold.

**4. We Meet at Ticonderoga (Lake George to Carillon, July 1758)**
- **History:** Duncan Campbell's death and the 42nd's assault. The 42nd also reached German Flatts after the 1757 raid, which gives a valley tie. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Campbell_(died_1758) , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_German_Flatts_(1757)
- **Play:** you are a sutler's hand or camp follower, or a ranger guide to the Highlanders. Campbell asks the locals where "Ticonderoga" is. In the days before the assault you carry out his errands: letters, a will, a cave in his story.
- **Folklore layer:** the cousin appears, and Campbell's dread is justified.
- **History mode:** the same chain plays as a man's premonition.
- **Ends:** in the assault as a set-piece, which you do not win.

**5. The Hymn at Carlisle (Pennsylvania, 1755 to December 1764). This ties the Prologue to Part 2.**
- **History:** Penn's Creek and Bouquet's return of captives. https://www.chasingfamilyghosts.com/post/a-curious-letter-part-four-facts-v-legend
- **Play:**
  - In the Prologue, a Palatine neighbour at German Flatts teaches the player a hymn, as a dialogue item.
  - In 1764 the player is an escort or interpreter at the captives' return. A German mother cannot find her daughter, and the hymn the player carries is the key.
  - Shows how "returned" captives were received.
- **Consultation:** the side of the adoptive families is a slot.
- **Needs:** a "song" journal item that can be used in dialogue.

**6. The Levellers (Dutchess County, 1766). The same story as The Ford, told from below.**
- **History:** https://history.nycourts.gov/case/crown-v-prendergast/
- **Play:**
  - Tenant night raids and turning out the landlord's new lessees.
  - The 28th Regiment marches. You can break captured leaders out of jail; this is unverified detail, so check it before using it.
  - The trial: Mehitable's courtroom defence as a dialogue battle.
  - Her winter ride to the governor as a timed horse run. Flag it as tradition, not record.
- **Consultation:** Daniel Nimham's Wappinger claim is a slot.
- **Fit:** reuses The Ford's court flow on the Albany and Hudson map.

**7. The Beech Seal (the New Hampshire Grants, 1770–74). A GTA-style gang chain that hands off to Part 4.**
- **History:** https://allthingsliberty.com/2013/03/the-legacy-of-ethan-allen/ , https://www.wehd.com/thornton/Beech_seal.html
- **Play:**
  - Ride with the Green Mountain Boys: chase off Yorker surveyors, burn a cabin or refuse to, and haul a Yorker up the tavern sign.
  - Or guard New York's surveyors as Hollis and Company's men.
  - 1774 puts a capital bounty on you, and May 1775 is Ticonderoga.
- **Needs:** a wanted or bounty level and two faction reputations.
- **Note:** both sides are land grabs, and the Abenaki's land is a slot.

**8. Lydius's Deeds / The Carrying Place (Fort Edward and Champlain, 1750s). A source for Silas Vane, or a smuggling chain of its own.**
- **History:** https://www.biographi.ca/en/bio/lydius_john_hendricks_4E.html , https://parks.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/fortchambly/activ/expositions-exhibitions/robert-sanders
- **Play:**
  - Before 1755, run woollens north and furs south on the lake. After war starts, it is treason.
  - Find out that the man selling you a deed bought it from one sachem out of council.
- **Arc Raiders analogue:** a cargo run through contested ground with a choice of where to extract, Albany or Montreal.
- **Consultation:** the Kahnawake carriers are a slot; French and Albany traders fill the roles until then.

**9. The Witch-Master (the Mountain Road, 1763–70s). A side chain that works with the folklore layer off or on.**
- **History:** Doddridge and the Essington witch bottle. https://www.heraldstaronline.com/opinion/local-columns/2023/10/history-in-the-hills-witches-of-cross-creek/ , https://archive.archaeology.org/online/features/halloween/witch_bottle.html
- **Play:**
  - A neighbour's rifle "won't shoot true," cattle sicken, and a child has fits. The witch-master names an old widow.
  - You can melt a silver button into a bullet and shoot her picture, bury a witch bottle, or find the real cause: a fouled barrel, a bad spring, mouldy rye.
  - You protect the widow or condemn her. The Cross Creek case shows how it can end.
- **Needs:** crafting (a silver bullet from a coin or button), the husbandry ailments the household already has, and reputation.

**10. Lights on the Line (the valley, inside The Ford, 1765–68)**
- **History and lore:** Grimm's cursed surveyors and the boundary-stone movers. https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/willowisp.html
- **Play:**
  - The farm family's grandmother says Irrlichter walk where a false line was run.
  - Lights move across the marsh square at night along the line Elias died over.
  - **Folklore layer:** follow them to the true corner stones, which become evidence for *The Magistrate*.
  - **History mode:** the lights are Vane's men with lanterns, moving stakes at night.
  - The same mission and layout work both ways.

**Bonus, for later years:**
- **Money-Diggers / Smith's Plunder (1779 onward).** A PvPvE treasure event. The rule "dig in silence or the treasure slips away" becomes a real mechanic: anyone who fires or shouts inside the circle loses the chest.
- **The Dark Day (19 May 1780).** A world event in "The Burning Valley" storyline. Shakers preach at Niskayuna, and panic and conversions follow.

## E. Design notes for the supernatural layer

- **A three-setting New Game option:** *History* (no supernatural), *Whispers* (ambiguous: omens, lights, dreams, every one with a mundane reading), and *Folklore* (the colonists' beliefs are true). Hooks 3, 4, 9 and 10 are written to play under all three.
- **Keep the magic close to the people who held it:** Palatine braucherei and Himmelsbriefe, Scots-Irish witch-masters, Highland second sight and ghosts, Dutch storm goblins and sleepers, treasure spirits, and Moravian and Shaker visions. Never Native beings.
- **Keep the 1757 voice clean.** Irving, Whittier and Skinner are later, so build from the older motifs (the mountain sleeper, the storm goblin, the burning ship) rather than quoting their texts.
- **Map suggestion:** add "The Lake" (Lake George, from Fort William Henry to Carillon) to `maps.md`. It carries hooks 1, 3 and 4, and part of 8, plus a Rangers storyline and PvPvE raids, without needing a Native town.
