# Thủy Vân: the game plan on one page

## What the game becomes

Thủy Vân replaces the 1765 frontier: its map, peoples and story. The frontier goes into the project's history, and the
engine stays: movement, climbing, boats, household, survival, the story system and co-op. You play a new, ordinary
person of any of your eight peoples. You live through Book I's five months, when the river at Đá Trắng stops and a war
starts for the wrong reason. You meet your canon cast but never take their place or make their choices. Nobody chosen,
and no side is the good one. All text is in English, and names keep their Vietnamese spelling with an English title
("Vạn Thủy, Capital of the Waters").

## What you can play first, and when

| When | What you play |
|---|---|
| ~3 weeks | **Still Water**: today's valley re-lit as a tropical early-rains morning. You play a Mountain Folk body with no guns. Press a key and one stretch of the river stops dead: still water, fish hanging, mist that doesn't move, no sound. You also pick the game's look from three pictures. |
| When the Tidebreak loop takes it | Tidebreak with the show's words gone: arts instead of elements, new move names |
| ~2 months | The real gorge at Đá Trắng and the Mountain village, built from new terrain |
| ~6-8 months | The story's opening: the Prologue and Act 1, as Mountain Folk |
| ~13-16 months | All of Book I, for Mountain Folk and Stone Children |

## Which peoples come first, and why

**Mountain Folk come first.** They have a human body, the most of your cast lives near them, and Book I starts on their
river. **Stone Children** come second. Dragon-Sea People, Ancestor-Bound and Hồ Tinh (in human form) appear early as
people you meet. **Riverborn** wait for swimming, which the game can't do yet, so their gills and webbed hands will do
something. **Floodborn** and **Mộc Tinh** get bodies of their own, built from grey-box shapes you approve first, so they
are never humans in costumes. Nobody gets stat bonuses, and no people is locked out of an art.

## Your story, in a few lines

You teach your child (or sibling, or ward) your people's rite, and that rite puts their name on the register. A month
later the river at Đá Trắng stops, your elder is lost below the still water, and the levy takes your child to a dig in
the south. The dig is burned, and the notice says "pirates" and "no one lived". The word "pirates" is the empire's claim,
never the game's. You live with that notice, carry what you saw in your own ledger, and bring your child home changed.
You stand somewhere when the war is declared on a false account. Your choices change your village, your family and who
trusts you. They never change the river or the war. Nothing in Book I spoils the Ninth River.

## Tidebreak's place

It is Vạn Thủy's gate game: the lock crews' sport, with basin matches all year and the final at the Flood-Rite days. The
venue is a prepared place, so the arts are huge there. Out in the world they stay small, useful and costly, and the war
stays one of spears and supply lines. The four kits become **Current, Stone, Hearth and Wind**: arts learned from
teachers, never a nation. Fighters wear their own people's clothes with a crew sash, and the show-like ring outfits and
the four element symbols go. Mẹ Lửa thinks the sport wastes what should be kept.

## What we decided for you (say no to any)

- Anyone can learn any art from a teacher, one art per person, and you can also start with no art. An art needs its
  material and gets weaker away from home. Its costs are debts and duties, never speed or aim.
- Soldiers fight with spear, bow and dao, not arts.
- Your child always survives Book I. The v6 switch to playing the child waits for a later book.
- If you go down, you wake where someone carried you, and you owe them. No respawning at your bed.
- A few horses for officials and soldiers; mules, oxen and buffalo for everyone else. No hunting foxes or crocodiles.
- Where your short stories and the V3 bible disagree, V3 wins.
- Nobody learns what the still water does to a hand or a boat in Book I. Watchers keep people back.

   fonts already on the computer.
3. **Places only you can name:**
   - Which crossing station is destroyed in month 5: **Khe Lan Ferry** (your story #27, and if so, what happens to

   - Which Sacred River is counted twice under a seasonal name, and which one is Đá Trắng's? (The game shows nine
     rivers by ritual name, never the map's numbered eight.)
